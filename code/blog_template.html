Every football fan has a version of the argument. Your club was massive once. Theirs only got big recently. Somebody's grandad saw sixty thousand at a ground that now holds twelve. Most of these arguments are about when, and the answer is usually hiding in old league tables and attendance records that nobody has put side by side.

So I did. James Curley's engsoccerdata package has every English league result since the first Saturday of the Football League in September 1888, {{data.n_matches|int}} matches across {{data.n_seasons|int}} seasons (I filled one missing season from openfootball). From those I rebuilt every final table, with the right points for a win and the right tie-breaks for each era. Then I joined each club's average home league crowd, season by season, from the remarkable collection at englishfootballleaguetables.co.uk. That gives {{data.n_clubs|int}} clubs, each with a place, a league position and a crowd, every season.

Twelve clubs from two places

The first season looks almost nothing like now. All twelve founders came from two places: six from Lancashire (Preston, Accrington, Blackburn, Bolton, Burnley and Everton) and six from the Midlands (Aston Villa, Derby, Notts County, Stoke, West Brom and Wolves). The biggest crowd was Everton's, {{crowd_kings.first.avg|int}} a game. London did not get a league club until {{london.first_season|raw}}, and did not get a champion until Arsenal in {{champions.first_london|raw}}.

{{founders.still_league|int}} of the twelve are still in the league. Only {{founders.still_top|int}}, Aston Villa, Everton and Wolves, were in the Premier League in {{crowds.last_season|raw}}.

{{x_peak_m}} million, and then the long fall

Crowds grew almost every season until the First World War, and again between the wars. Then came the extraordinary seasons after 1945, when a country short of almost everything else went to the football. In {{crowds.peak_season|raw}} league matches drew {{x_peak_m}} million people, an average of {{crowds.peak_avg|int}} a game across all four divisions. It has never been matched.

What followed was one of the longest declines in British leisure. The turn came the very next season, before Hillsborough, before all-seater grounds and before the Premier League, and it has kept coming: {{x_last_m}} million in {{crowds.last_season|raw}}, {{x_last_vs_peak}} of the peak. Only {{crowds.n_seasons_beating_last|int}} seasons, all between {{crowds.first_season_beating_last|raw}} and {{crowds.last_season_beating_last|raw}}, beat it.

The biggest crowd in the land

Everton had the biggest crowds in the league's first decade, then Aston Villa, then Newcastle and Chelsea took turns before 1914. Arsenal owned the 1930s. Since {{crowd_kings.man_utd_since|raw}} every recorded season has belonged to Manchester United, {{x_mu_kings}} seasons in all, peaking at {{crowd_kings.biggest.avg|int}} in {{crowd_kings.biggest.season|raw}}.

{{lost.n|int}} towns that lost league football

{{lost.n_towns_ever|int}} towns and cities have had a league club at some point. {{lost.n_towns_last|int}} have one in {{lost.today|raw}}. The other {{lost.n|int}} lost it and have not got it back. Some are footnotes from the 1890s: Darwen, Bootle, Northwich, and Glossop, who spent a single season in the top flight. Some went between the wars, from mining and mill towns: Merthyr Tydfil, Aberdare, Ashington, Nelson, Durham, Stalybridge. And {{lost.since_2000|int}} have gone since 2000, Bury most painfully, expelled in 2019 after {{lost.most_seasons.seasons|int}} seasons. Rochdale, Hartlepool, Southend and Scunthorpe are among the recent ones, and Carlisle and Morecambe dropped out at the end of {{crowds.last_season|raw}}. It can be undone: Barrow came back in 2020 after 48 years away, and Barnet and Oldham are back in League Two for {{lost.today|raw}}.

When was your club big?

The best part of the interactive version is the search: type any club and you get its whole history as a sparkline of league position and crowd. Every club has a peak, and they are scattered across the whole history of the game. Of the {{peaks.n_clubs_20|int}} clubs with twenty or more league seasons, {{peaks.by_decade.1890s|int}} had their best decade in the 1890s, and {{peaks.by_decade.2020s|int}} are living through it now. Preston's was the very first season. Huddersfield's was the 1920s, Portsmouth's the late 1940s, Brighton's and Bournemouth's the last ten years.

How I did this

Tables come from every league match in engsoccerdata (2022-23 from openfootball): two points for a win until 1981, three after; goal average until 1976, then goal difference; points deductions applied; expunged records removed. I checked the rebuilt champions against the record. Crowds are average home league gates from englishfootballleaguetables.co.uk, matched to clubs season by season through all the old names (Small Heath, Newton Heath, The Wednesday and the rest). Season totals are average times home league games. Each club sits at its present or last ground, so early Arsenal is in Islington rather than Plumstead. Crowds before the mid 1920s are estimates, and there are none for 2020-21 (behind closed doors) or 2021-22 (missing from my copy of the source). The paper has the details.
